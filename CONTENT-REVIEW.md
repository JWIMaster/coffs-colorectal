### Specific clinical claims worth a second look

These are the statements most likely to need local adjustment:

- [ ] **Risks wording.** Every procedure page lists risks, including rare but
      serious ones. Confirm the list matches the practice's usual consent
      discussion, and that nothing is missing that Dr Sutherland would always
      mention.
- [ ] **Recovery timelines.** Laparoscopic "3–4 days", open "8–10 days", "6 weeks
      no heavy lifting", "12–24 months" for bowel function after rectal surgery.
      These come from published sources; confirm they match local experience.
- [ ] **Medicines before colonoscopy.** The bowel preparation page says blood
      thinners, diabetes medicines, iron and anti-diarrhoeals need review, and
      that blood pressure medicines usually continue. Confirm this matches the
      instructions the practice and the hospitals actually give.
- [ ] **Bowel preparation instructions.** The page describes the usual Australian
      approach but does not name a product, because the TGA prohibits advertising
      prescription medicines. Confirm the timing described matches the practice's
      own written instructions, and that patients are always given the practice's
      instructions as well.
- [ ] **The "around 7%" figure** for inadequate bowel preparation is attributed to
      Bowel Cancer Australia. Keep, soften or remove as preferred.
- [ ] **ERAS pathway.** The enhanced recovery page describes carbohydrate drinks,
      early feeding and early mobilisation. Confirm this is the pathway used at
      Baringa Private Hospital and Coffs Harbour Health Campus.
- [ ] **Pilonidal surgery.** The page refers to an open-healing technique and to
      Dr Paul Kitchen, carried over from the original resources page. Confirm this
      is still accurate and that the reference is still wanted.
- [ ] **Acknowledgement of Country.** Currently names the Gumbaynggirr people.
      Confirm the wording the practice prefers. CSSANZ publishes model wording.

## 4. Legal and advertising compliance

These were enforced during the build. Please confirm the practice is comfortable
with each, because responsibility for advertising sits with the practitioner.

- [ ] **No testimonials anywhere** — required by s133(1)(c) of the National Law.
      This includes not adding review widgets or Google review badges later.
- [ ] **No comparative or superiority claims** — no "best", "leading" or "most
      experienced".
- [ ] **No named bowel preparation products** — the TGA prohibits advertising
      prescription medicines to the public.
- [ ] **No before/after or clinical images.**
- [ ] **No fear-based urgency.** Symptom information is framed neutrally, with
      "see your GP" rather than "don't wait — it could be cancer".
- [ ] **Contact form warning.** The form carries a collection notice and an
      explicit instruction not to send clinical information, because it is not
      monitored outside business hours. Confirm the practice will monitor whatever
      inbox it points to, and that replies will be by telephone rather than by
      email for anything clinical.
