## Definition

The colon is another name for the large bowel excluding the rectum. A cancer is a malignant growth of tissue. Malignant means that has the potential to spread to other areas of the body. It does NOT mean that it has already spread. The term “bowel cancer” refers to cancer of the colon or the rectum. Despite many similarities between the rectum and the colon there are significant differences in the way cancer is treated between these two areas. It is therefore important to clarify where the cancer is exactly.

## Diagnosis

Colon cancer is usually readily diagnosed by colonoscopy and biopsy (removal of a very small part of the tumour for testing).

Screening tests, such as the bowel scan (faecal occult blood test or FOBT), detect microscopic amounts of blood in the faeces. They do not detect cancer. Many cancers bleed a small amount into the bowel but there are many other causes for a positive screening test apart from cancer.

Following diagnosis further tests are usually performed to determine the stage of the cancer. This is largely aimed at detecting spread to other organs and involves a CT or CAT scan of the abdomen and chest.

## Treatment

The main treatment for colon cancer is surgery to remove the area of colon with the cancer and also the lymph glands around that area. The bowel can usually be rejoined and there is usually no need for a bag

## Follow up

There are several reasons why your surgeon will continue to see you after surgery. Initially it is important to ensure that the recovery proceeds well. In the longer term it is important to be monitored for recurrence of the cancer. This will involve regular blood tests and visits for 5 years after the time of surgery. Recurrence of a cancer may still be curable if it is detected early.

In addition there is an ongoing risk of developing more polyps in the bowel or even a second bowel cancer. Colonoscopy will be recommended periodically depending on the nature of the initial cancer.

The diagnosis of cancer and the treatment that it requires are often devestating to an individual and their family. There are many supports available to help during this difficult period and afterwards. Speak to your surgeon or general practitioner for help.
