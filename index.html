Bowel cancer screening saves lives

Bowel cancer is one of the most common cancers in Australia, and it is one of the most treatable when it is found early. The National Bowel Cancer Screening Program posts a free test kit to eligible Australians every two years.

  • People aged 50 to 74 are sent a free kit automatically every two years.
  • People aged 45 to 49 can request their first free kit, and are then sent one every two years. Call the National Cancer Screening Register on 1800 627 701 or visit ncsr.gov.au.
  • If you are younger than 45 or older than 74, or you have a family history of bowel cancer, speak to your GP about what screening is right for you.

The test looks for invisible traces of blood in a bowel motion. A positive result does not mean you have cancer — it means you need a colonoscopy to find out why the blood is there. If you already have symptoms, see your GP rather than waiting for a screening kit; screening is for people without symptoms.

Symptoms worth checking

Most people with these symptoms do not have cancer, but they should be checked by a GP.

  • Blood in your bowel motion, or on the toilet paper
  • A change in bowel habit lasting more than a few weeks
  • Unexplained weight loss
  • Abdominal pain, bloating or a feeling of not emptying your bowel
  • Unexplained anaemia or low iron
