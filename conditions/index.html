Conditions

Each page explains a condition in plain language: what it is, what causes it, the symptoms, how it is diagnosed, and the treatments available, starting with the treatments that do not involve surgery. Most colorectal conditions are managed without an operation.

Colon cancer Cancer of the colon, the large bowel above the rectum. Usually found by colonoscopy, and usually treated by removing the affected section of bowel along with the nearby lymph glands. Also called bowel cancer, or large bowel cancer Rectal cancer Cancer of the rectum, the last part of the large bowel before the anus. Treatment often involves chemotherapy and radiotherapy before surgery, and sometimes a temporary stoma. Also called bowel cancer, or cancer of the rectum Diverticular disease Diverticular disease is when small pouches form in the wall of the large bowel. Most cause no trouble, but inflammation can cause pain, fever and bleeding. Includes diverticulosis and diverticulitis Crohn disease Crohn's disease is a long-term condition where the body's immune system inflames the gut, causing pain and diarrhoea. Treatment aims to control it, not cure it. Also called Crohn's disease or IBD Ulcerative colitis Ulcerative colitis is a long-term condition that inflames the lining of the large bowel, causing diarrhoea and urgency. Medicines control it well for most people. Also called colitis, or IBD Haemorrhoids Haemorrhoids are swollen cushions of tissue in and around the anus. Most settle with simple measures, but bleeding from the back passage should always be checked. Also called piles Anal fissure An anal fissure is a small tear in the lining of the anus that causes sharp pain and bleeding when you pass a stool. Most heal with simple care. A tear in the lining of the anus Anal abscess and fistula An anal abscess is a painful collection of pus near the back passage that needs prompt drainage. A fistula is a tunnel that can form afterwards. Two related problems: abscess and fistula Pilonidal sinus Pilonidal disease causes a small tunnel or lump in the crease between the buttocks. It only needs treatment if it becomes infected or keeps coming back. Also called pilonidal disease or a pilonidal cyst

Making an appointment

A referral from your general practitioner is needed to claim a Medicare rebate for a specialist consultation.

Call (02) 6651 6211 How to get a referral

In an emergency, call 000. For urgent advice when the rooms are closed, call healthdirect on 1800 022 222, free, 24 hours. For life-threatening symptoms go to Coffs Harbour Health Campus emergency department.
